The dataset in numbers

Train and test are split by schema, so no test schema is seen in training.

Abstract

Frontier language models in agentic pipelines lead text-to-SQL benchmarks, at a high inference cost. Small specialised models would avoid that cost, but training them needs data that reflects the scale, semantics and structure of real databases. SQaLe is a semi-synthetic text-to-SQL dataset built on 9,259 real-world schemas from SchemaPile. Our generation pipeline extends each schema, fills it with synthetic rows, generates questions and answers them with an agent, and validates every stage by execution.

The result is 1,408,056 natural-language questions paired with SQL, over schemas far larger than in any existing dataset. We train Qwen3.5-2B on SQaLe with GRPO alone, and its execution accuracy on the SQaLe test set rises from 24.0% to 54.7%. The model learns to explore the database: it finds the relevant tables in large schemas and reads the values its answer needs, which training on existing datasets elicits far less.

How it is built

Five stages, each validated by execution. Tags name the paper's design criteria.

The pipeline starts from schemas that people wrote for real applications and keeps their naming, structure and domain. Every later stage works against a live, populated database.

How it compares

Results

One recipe, three training corpora.

We train Qwen3.5-2B with GRPO directly from the base checkpoint three times: on SQaLe, on SynSQL-2.5M and on BIRD train. Base model, environment, tools, reward, steps, batch size and evaluation are identical. There are no distilled traces, no teacher and no test-time scaffolding.

The schema is withheld. The model explores the database through tools: the five above plus foreign_keys and join_path. The reward is tiered (correct result, then executes, then parses, then nothing), with partial credit that never ranks a wrong query above a correct one.

Qwen3.5-2B trained with GRPO on SQaLe, MSQaLe, reaches 54.7% on the SQaLe test set, 3.6 points behind Qwen3.6-27B. Its gain over the base model is 30.7 points, against 19.7 for MBIRD and 13.3 for MSynSQL.

What SQaLe teaches

Three properties of the data show up in how the trained models behave. All three figures use the SQaLe test set with the schema withheld.

Schema size

Accuracy falls for every model as the schema grows, and MSQaLe leads at every size. At full schema size it inspects 71.3% of the gold tables, against 56.5% for MBIRD and 60.2% for MSynSQL. BIRD and SynSQL training schemas have a median of 5 and 10 tables, so models trained on them never have to search.

Populated tables

Before submitting, MSQaLe has seen 75% of the string literals its answer depends on. MSynSQL has seen 45%, MBIRD 36% and the untrained model 25%. SynSQL's tables hold a median of 2 rows; SQaLe's hold 69.

Domains

On SQaLe test questions whose schemas lie outside BIRD's domains, MSQaLe leads MBIRD by 9.9 points (46.4 vs 36.5). Inside BIRD's domains it leads by 2.8 points (39.3 vs 36.5).

The sample covers 108 schemas, too few to call the difference between the two gaps significant.
